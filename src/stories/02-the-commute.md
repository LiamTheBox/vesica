# The Commute

By the third stop, the bus had a smell (wet umbrellas, somebody's breakfast burrito) and Mara had a plan. The plan was to think about anything else.

She thought about the client's lobby drawings. She thought about whether the new intern had been joking about the pineapple pizza. She thought about the pole she was holding and how many hands had held it, which was a mistake, so she went back to the lobby drawings.

Then the driver announced, cheerfully, that roadworks on Harbor Street meant a detour.

The detour had potholes. Not ordinary potholes, but the kind with ambitions. The first one lifted the whole back of the bus and dropped it again, and Mara's knees locked on reflex. Every muscle she owned below the waist reported for duty at once.

*Okay,* she thought. *Okay. That's fine. We're fine.*

The second pothole she saw coming. She braced, squeezed, breathed out slowly through her nose like the yoga app told her to, and it passed. She felt absurdly proud of herself.

The third pothole she did not see, because a woman beside her chose that exact moment to sneeze, enormously, into her elbow, and Mara, who had never in her life been able to hear a sneeze without catching one, felt her own nose start to tickle.

No. Absolutely not.

She pressed her tongue to the roof of her mouth. She stared hard at a poster for dental insurance. The tickle built, wavered, built again. She clenched everything she had and let the sneeze go into her sleeve. For one long, terrible half-second, her whole body was a dam with a crack in it.

The dam held.

"Bless you," said the woman.

"Thanks," Mara said, slightly higher than usual.

They crawled along the detour behind a cement mixer. Mara shifted her weight from one foot to the other and back, the slow, casual sway of someone who was definitely just enjoying the ride. The travel mug was empty now. She had no memory of finishing it.

At last, the office tower slid into view, glass and steel and, somewhere on the fourth floor, a bathroom with her name on it. The bus stopped. The doors opened. Mara was first off, walking with the very specific briskness of a person who must not run.

The lobby. The badge reader. The elevator, which took its time like it always did.

Fourth floor. She rounded the corner to the restroom.

A yellow sign stood in the doorway: *CLEANING IN PROGRESS. BACK IN 20 MIN.*

Behind her, her boss's voice called down the hall. "Mara! Perfect timing. They're early. Conference room B."
