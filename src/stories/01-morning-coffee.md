# Morning Coffee

Mara Quinn's alarm had a snooze button, and Mara Quinn had opinions about it. By the time she won the argument, it was 7:52, the 8:10 bus was a rumor, and her apartment smelled of the double espresso she'd started on autopilot.

She drank it standing up. Then, because the day ahead held a site visit, a client review and her boss's favorite phrase, *quick sync*, she filled the big travel mug too. Twenty-eight years old, a junior architect with a corner of a desk she was allowed to call hers, and still running on caffeine and optimism.

Keys. Badge. Laptop. One shoe, then, after a search under the couch, the other.

She was halfway down the stairs when the thought arrived, polite and small: *you didn't go*.

Mara stopped on the landing. Upstairs was her bathroom, three flights and a stubborn lock away. Downstairs was the street, and somewhere beyond it, the bus. She did the math the way she did everything before nine: badly, and in her own favor. Twenty minutes on the bus. Five minutes' walk. The office had perfectly good bathrooms. She'd be fine.

The travel mug, warm in her hand, said nothing. It didn't have to.

Outside, the morning was bright and loud. A street sweeper hissed past. Somewhere a café was grinding beans, and the smell followed her to the stop like a dare. She took a sip, because she was not going to waste good coffee, and checked the board.

*Next bus: 6 min.*

Six minutes. Fine. She stood with her bag over one shoulder and watched the traffic, and somewhere around minute four her body filed its first formal notice. Not urgent. Just a gentle, interested pressure, the way a cat looks at you when it's almost dinnertime.

"Noted," Mara said, quietly, to nobody.

A man in a running jacket glanced over. She smiled the bright, professional smile she saved for clients and pretended to read the timetable.

The bus came at minute eight, packed to the doors. Mara squeezed on, found a pole, and held it. The doors sighed shut. The bus lurched. The coffee sloshed in its mug, and something else sloshed in solidarity.

Twenty minutes, she told herself. She had done twenty minutes a hundred times.

She just usually hadn't started them with two coffees on board.
