# The Concert Queue

The festival was Priya's idea. Priya had been Mara's best friend since their first week of college, when they'd bonded over a broken vending machine and a shared hatred of eight a.m. lectures, and Priya's ideas had a way of sounding easy at brunch and turning into adventures by evening.

"Outdoor stage, golden hour, our favorite band," she'd said. "What could go wrong?"

What could go wrong turned out to be the lemonade. It came in giant souvenir cups shaped like pineapples, it was exactly cold enough and exactly sweet enough, and it was very, very large.

At six thirty, an hour before the headliners, Mara looked at the portable toilets at the edge of the field. There were twelve of them. The line in front of them wound past a food truck and twice around a lamppost.

"Later," she decided. "When it's quieter."

It did not get quieter.

By seven fifteen, the crowd had packed in tight in front of the stage, and Priya had found them a spot near the front with a clear view. Mara's pineapple cup was empty. Her bladder had opinions about this, and was sharing them.

"I should go now," she said. "Before they start."

Priya looked at the line, which now had its own weather system. "You'll miss the first half of the set."

The lights went down. A cheer went up. The first chord rang out across the field, the one from the song they'd sung in Priya's car a thousand times, and Mara felt the decision make itself.

"I'll go after this one," she said.

She did not go after that one. She stayed through the second song, and the third, bouncing on her toes, which helped with the music and with other things. She sang along to every chorus and squeezed through every bass drop. The bass drops were the worst. The low notes went right through her, a deep vibration that made every muscle she was holding tight want to give up and take a nap.

Then the singer told a story between songs about getting lost on tour in a town with no gas stations, and the crowd roared with laughter, and Priya laughed so hard she grabbed Mara's arm, and Mara had to fold slightly at the waist, knees together, and laugh *very carefully* while holding on.

"You okay?" Priya shouted over the noise.

"Never better," Mara shouted back, which was a lie, but a festive one.

She made it to the encore. She made it through the encore. When the last chord faded and the lights came up, she handed Priya her empty pineapple cup and said, with great dignity, "Save my place in life."

The line had thinned to merely enormous. Mara joined it and stood very still, one hand on the lamppost, breathing in slow counts. Twenty-six people ahead. Then twenty. Somebody's friend cut in, and the whole line groaned as one.

When the plastic door finally banged shut behind her, Mara made a solemn private vow about souvenir lemonade.

Outside, Priya was waiting with two bottles of water and a grin.

"Hydration," she said. "Pace yourself. We've got the road trip on Saturday."
